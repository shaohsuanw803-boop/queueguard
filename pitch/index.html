How QueueGuard works

Sense. Predict. Act. Guide.

1 · Sense

Radar speeds every 20 s, every 400 m

2 · Predict

Where the queue tail will be in 90 s

3 · Act

Colour VMS at stopping distance · VSL 100 → 80 → 60 · worker and truck alerts

4 · Guide

A plan for each site, tested in a virtual trial

Fail-safe: no data for 60 s → every board shows PREPARE TO STOP · a person confirms every truck broadcast

10

(1:55–2:38) Here's how our prototype works in simulation. First, radar sensors, spaced 400 metres apart, report speeds every 20 seconds. Next, it predicts where the queue tail will be in 90 seconds. Then, it selects a warning board before the predicted queue, allowing for stopping distance, and lowers speed limits in stages. The design includes worker phone alerts and supervisor-confirmed radio warnings for truck drivers. Finally, it creates a site plan for a virtual trial before deployment. If data stops for 60 seconds, the boards switch to "Prepare to stop." Let's see it in action. [hand over; click to start the demo] 中文：下面是原型在仿真中的运行方式。首先，每隔 400 米设置的雷达，每 20 秒报告车速。接着，预测 90 秒后的队尾位置。然后，系统考虑停车距离，选择预计队尾前方合适的警示牌，并逐级降低限速。方案包括工作人员手机提醒，以及主管确认后向卡车司机发出的无线电预警。最后，生成现场方案，先在虚拟环境测试。如果连续 60 秒没有数据，警示牌切换为“准备停车”。接下来看看它如何运行。[交给队友，翻页播放 Demo。] Presenter note: phone delivery and manual sign override are planned pilot integrations, as explained in appendix A3/A4; the current prototype models these workflows in simulation.

(3:25–4:05) So — does it work? We ran it 40 times, with the same drivers for every option. We counted serious near misses at the back of the queue, using Transport for NSW's yardstick. Static signs: 0.72 an hour. A standard fixed-position system: 0.67 — no statistically clear difference in our simulation. QueueGuard: 0.23. [pause] That's 68 percent fewer. The fastest drivers reach the queue 14 km/h slower, the queue is never left unwarned, and traffic flows just as well. A3 · Control logic

Simple rules a traffic engineer can audit.

Detect20 s mean speed below 35 km/h for two periods = queued; above 55 km/h to clear
LocateTail interpolated between radars 400 m apart
PredictTail speed fitted by least squares over the last 180 s, projected 90 s ahead
PlaceFirst board at least 300 m upstream of the predicted tail: STOPPED TRAFFIC / PREPARE TO STOP, flashing; boards further upstream: QUEUE AHEAD x KM
Slow80 within 2.1 km of the risk point, 60 within 900 m; neighbouring signs differ by at most 20 km/h; relax only after a 60 s hold
AlertCrew alert when a vehicle passes a radar within 450 m of the tail above 80 km/h (dashboard now, phones in the pilot) · UHF CB 40 message drafted, a person confirms
TrustEscalate at once, relax only after a 60 s hold; no queue message without a detected queue
Fail-safeNo data for 60 s: every board PREPARE TO STOP, VSL 80

A3

Use if asked how it decides, how it avoids flicker or false alarms, or what happens when comms drop. Warning offset, hold time and prediction horizon are settings; the other thresholds are engineering defaults that shadow mode will calibrate against real radar data.

Use if a judge pushes on "it's only a simulation" or on prediction accuracy. Say it plainly: these are relative comparisons under stated assumptions, not a forecast of real crash reductions. That is exactly why the first step is shadow mode, which changes nothing on the road.

Every site gets a plan — and a virtual trial.

In

Out

This site's speed, demand and truck share on our standard closure, static signs against QueueGuard, 6 seeds: −50% severe conflicts in this example

Printable for the traffic management plan; a planning aid a qualified designer verifies

A5

Use if asked about the "set of guidelines" in the brief, or how it adapts to a new site.
